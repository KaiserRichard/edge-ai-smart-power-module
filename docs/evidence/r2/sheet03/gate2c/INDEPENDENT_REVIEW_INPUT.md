## Actual topology

**OBSERVATION:** This is **common-source**. Both series sources and U301 TS meet at PROT_MID. Q301 drain connects to VOUT_5V1 and Q302 drain to VOUT_PROTECTED. Each N-channel body diode points **from the middle source node toward its outer drain**. When both channels are reliably off, there is no pair of forward-biased body diodes connecting either outer rail to the other. The pair blocks DC through-conduction in either direction, subject to leakage, capacitive displacement and voltage ratings. When enabled, the channels conduct in either direction; this is not active reverse-current protection during normal operation.

The historical description at architecture line 96 is common-drain and is not the present drawing. An opposed common-drain pair could also block both directions when off, but its sources are separate; applying this common-source driver wiring and shared gate reference to that different topology would not be an interchangeable implementation.

## Crowbar pulse, eFuse action and sequencing

**DATASHEET:** CSD18540Q5B specifies a 400 A pulse headline subject to pulse/duty/SOA conditions and maximum junction-to-case resistance 0.8 K/W. It supplies SOA/thermal curves, not an I²t fuse-style rating. **ASSUMED:** device initial temperature 125°C, protected node no higher than the required ceiling, isolation within the timing budget, downstream C no greater than 220 µF and no independently powered load.

Within that assumed ceiling, my load-line arithmetic gives **CALCULATED:** current upper bound 169.205 A and MOSFET power upper bound 232.657 W. Independent initial-interval simulation at the zero-RDS sensitivity gives **CALCULATED:** peak 161.693 A and I²t approximately 0.04262 A²s. Zero RDS is an **ASSUMED sensitivity**, not a manufacturer minimum.

For the isolated downstream store, `I²t = C × V² / (2R)` gives **CALCULATED conservative upper screen:** 0.102369 A²s using the specified assumed capacitance ceiling and minimum ballast. The sum of initial and upper-tail screens is **CALCULATED:** approximately 0.145 A²s; maxima need not coincide, so this is a conservative comparison. The initial-interval figure must not be read as total fault I²t.

Each ballast element sees **CALCULATED:** no more than 232.657 W under the assumed voltage ceiling and independent resistor corners. An **ASSUMED:** 100 µs rectangular extension gives **CALCULATED:** 0.023266 J per element. The WFM single-pulse graph provides **DATASHEET graphical reference**, not a tabulated hot-temperature guaranteed limit; the owner's deliberately lowered graph reading is **ASSUMED screen:** 1000 W at that duration. Applying the continuous-rating temperature derating line to pulses is also an **ASSUMED interpretation**. It produces **CALCULATED:** 450 W and 0.045 J at the assumed initial temperature, approximately 1.93 times the rectangle. I find no arithmetic reason to reject the resistor selection within this restricted envelope, but the thermal applicability and bounded envelope must stay open.

Likewise the MOSFET thermal screen uses an **ASSUMED conservative reading:** normalized transient impedance 0.05 from a typical curve; `232.657 × 0.8 × 0.05` gives **CALCULATED:** about 9.31 K rise. That is useful preliminary evidence, not a guaranteed hot SOA result for a layout not yet realized.

If the raw bank remains connected for longer than assumed, its **CALCULATED upper store screen:** 5.4144 mF and 0.081893 J at the assumed voltage ceiling must join the downstream/source/inductor energy. Its bulk-only I²t screen becomes **CALCULATED:** 2.519 A²s at minimum ballast, with a much longer tail. This is why assumed isolation cannot be replaced by the eFuse's slower overload latch timeout in the pulse argument.

The intended eFuse sequence is credible for maintained auxiliary power: `CROWBAR_ON` drives Q304, which pulls U101 SHDN low. **DATASHEET:** TPS2663 Rev G specifies shutdown-entry maximum 1.5 µs after SHDN crosses its threshold. The captured MODE pin is open, selecting latch behavior for overload/thermal faults. Its programmed current-limit corner is **CALCULATED:** roughly 4.181–4.820 A from the **DATASHEET:** 4.185–4.815 A test limits and resistor tolerance. Current limiting does not remove stored downstream energy or guarantee instantaneous isolation of the input capacitors. Opening via externally held SHDN is also not the same thing as having already reached the eFuse's internal overload latch. If Q304 releases SHDN during a brownout, an internal latch state cannot be assumed unless its qualifying fault occurred. [TPS2663 Rev G](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2663.pdf).

Actual logic, read from the netlist:

- `CTRL_ENABLE = SUPPLY_OK AND RESET_N`.
- `QUALIFIED = CTRL_ENABLE AND OV_OK AND BUCK_PGOOD`.
- U304 has D high, asynchronous clear controlled by QUALIFIED, Q=ARM_LATCH and /Q=ARM_LATCH_N.
- U309 samples ARM_LATCH on the delayed ARM edge; CTRL_ENABLE clears it.
- `RUN_GATE = ARM_LATCH AND ARM_HISTORY AND QUALIFIED`.
- `CROWBAR_ON = ARM_HISTORY AND ARM_LATCH_N AND CTRL_ENABLE`.

A cold, correctly reset start therefore inhibits both power switches and crowbar until qualification and a deliberate ARM event. RESET intentionally clears history and inhibits the outputs. The delayed history clock avoids the simple initial /Q transient crowbar problem. However, no logic power-on guarantee exists below specified supply conditions, and slow/partial power ramps still need review.

Once armed, comparator failure or BUCK_PGOOD loss clears the ARM latch while retaining history, so the crowbar fires if CTRL_ENABLE stays high. Thus it can fire on an ordinary undervoltage/PGOOD excursion as well as overvoltage. It can false-fire on load release if the unqualified normal-voltage bound is exceeded, or on ARM inrush if PGOOD falls. During brownout it may briefly fire while PGOOD falls before SUPPLY_OK; when SUPPLY_OK falls, it is forcibly disabled and history erased. These are logical consequences, not measured waveforms.

**DATASHEET:** TPS7A16 PG falling trip spans 83–93% of nominal. Combining the owner's regulated-minimum screens with the lower ratio gives **CALCULATED conservative screening values:** DET_5V about 4.051 V and DRV_7V5 about 6.175 V before PG must assert. This combination is an engineering screen, not a guarantee that independent corners coexist. It nevertheless shows PG cannot enforce the required regulated minimum. At the lower driver voltage, the owner's resistive gate-charge approximation changes from the nominal screening budget to **CALCULATED:** roughly 211 ns for charge alone, before comparator/logic/driver delays. **ASSUMED:** its gate-resistance/charge transfer between datasheet test conditions remains unqualified. The voltage safety inequality therefore needs explicit supply-qualified timing.
