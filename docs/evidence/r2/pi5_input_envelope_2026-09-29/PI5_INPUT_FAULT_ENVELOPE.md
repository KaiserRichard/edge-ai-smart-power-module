# Raspberry Pi 5 input fault envelope — BRIEF-03

Review date: **2026-09-29**. Scope: Raspberry Pi 5 USB-C power input and physical 5 V header pins 2/4, relative to board ground. “GPIO feeding” here means those power pins, not the 3.3 V signal pins. This is public-source engineering evidence, not a hardware test or a released electrical specification.

**P4 remains OPEN. R2 schematic release and R3 PCB release remain BLOCKED. NOT FOR FABRICATION — ENGINEERING REVIEW REQUIRED.**

## Result in five lines

- **KNOWN —** A Raspberry Pi engineer states that Pi 5 accepts 4.75–5.5 V and that noise/transients must not exceed 5.5 V; the discussion concerns USB power. This is operating guidance, not a destructive-stress rating. [S04](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?p=2157077#p2157077)
- **KNOWN —** USB-C has input overvoltage protection (OVP); the GPIO power path bypasses that USB-C protection, according to Raspberry Pi engineer jdb. No guaranteed trip threshold, delay or peak let-through is given. [S05](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?p=2194239)
- **INFERRED —** For GPIO, apply an interim ceiling of 5.5 V including overshoot at the board rail, with engineering margin; this extends the engineer guidance to the shared load rail, not a separately published GPIO survival rating.
- **ASSUMED —** The brief’s 6.0 V screen is not an accepted Pi 5 rating and grants no allowed exposure time. It must not close P4.
- **UNKNOWN —** No official Pi 5 voltage-versus-time fault-survival specification was found for either entry point; DA9091 input absolute maximum, transient allowance, and the actual weakest board component remain unknown.

**Weakest-limit answer:** The AP22652W6-7 has the **lowest retrieved numerical supply absolute maximum, +6.0 V**, among the identified semiconductor parts for which an applicable datasheet was obtained. This is a candidate component ceiling, conditional on its rail connection and fitted variant. **The part that actually sets the weakest Pi 5 board limit cannot be identified from these sources.** DA9091 or another exposed part may impose a lower limit. The 30 V MOSFET does not establish connector survivability; nor does the AP22652 value establish a safe 6 V pulse.

The MOSFET avalanche conditions on p. 2 include 0.1 mH, 22 A and low repetition/duty keeping junction temperature at 25°C. Its safe-operating-area graph is a transistor current/voltage/thermal constraint. There is no basis for converting it into Pi fault energy or time. [S07](https://www.diodes.com/datasheet/download/DMG7430LFG.pdf)

## Voltage versus time: what can actually be used

Define `V_C(t)` at USB-C VBUS to board ground and `V_R(t)` at the internal/header 5 V rail to board ground. Header-fed input directly applies `V_R(t)`; USB-C protection makes `V_C(t)` and `V_R(t)` potentially different during a fault. Cable/source voltage is not a substitute for the board-terminal waveform.

| Voltage/time case | USB-C | GPIO 5 V header | Evidence state / engineering consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal 5 V-class supply | Official 5 V / 5 A class; engineer 4.75–5.5 V guidance includes transients. | Official Power HAT+ guidance supports 5.1 V, preferably 5 A. | **KNOWN** normal guidance; not a complete undervoltage or reliability specification. |
| `V > 5.5 V`, however short | No allowed exposure duration found. Built-in OVP may isolate the rail; quantitative performance unavailable. | No allowed exposure duration found; USB-C OVP does not intervene. | **UNKNOWN** survival. Do not create an acceptance point for nanoseconds, microseconds or milliseconds. |
| 6.0 V screen | No Pi 5 board rating supports it. | Same. | **ASSUMED** historical/task screen only. A component absolute maximum is not a board qualification. |
| 9 / 15 / 20 V fault | These voltages explain the stated purpose of USB-C OVP; no guaranteed survival time or fault waveform supplied. | No protection credit from USB-C OVP. | **KNOWN** protection purpose; **UNKNOWN** connector/rail survival. |
| Negative voltage, repetitive pulses, allowed excess charge/energy | No quantified board envelope obtained. | Same. | **UNKNOWN**; no symmetry or energy equivalence assumed. |

**DECISION / INFERRED interim design constraint:** Evaluate protection against a board-rail ceiling `V_R,peak ≤ 5.5 V`, including ripple, overshoot and measurement uncertainty, pending conductor review and a formal requirement. Keep USB-C input itself within the available operating guidance. This is a no-exceed design constraint, **not** a claim that every waveform below it is safe. Margin, temperature, board revision, startup state and external peripherals still require review. For `V > 5.5 V`, `t_allowed(V)` is **UNKNOWN**, not a measured zero and not a guessed finite time. No survival curve is plotted because there are no defensible fault-duration points.

## USB-C versus GPIO

**KNOWN, engineer testimony:** jdb’s original posts on 16 February 2024 describe an inline USB-C FET that initially prevents input reaching the board until detection is active, then explicitly state that OVP applies only to USB-C. GPIO power bypasses this path. The same discussion describes startup sensitivity and unspecified debounce; those observations do not supply an OVP response time. [S05](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?p=2194239)

**INFERRED:** Both entry points ultimately energize the board’s 5 V loads, but USB-C has an intervening protection element. Without the full schematic, do not assign exact FET orientation, controller pins, all reverse-current paths, or a TVS topology. The USB peripheral switch identified in S08 must not be mistaken for a 5 A input protection switch. A software PD/current setting changes neither the hardware voltage rating nor the GPIO bypass. This review does not qualify simultaneous sources.

## Rejected extrapolations and unavailable sources

- **6.0 V from Pi 4:** The June 2024 OVP discussion explicitly guesses a Pi 5 value from Pi 4. It is not a Pi 5 datasheet. Similarly, Pi 4’s SMBJ5.0A-TR TVS identification cannot be transferred to Pi 5. [Discussion](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=372663)
- **PMIC anecdotes:** jamesh’s 30 July 2024 posts state that Raspberry Pi cannot publish the DA9091 datasheet and clarify that the reported 9 V nanosecond PMIC failure concerns **Pi 4**. Neither that anecdote nor another user’s 5.7 V interpretation establishes Pi 5 survival. [S10](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?p=2241067)
- **Similar Renesas chips:** DA9061, DA9090 and DA9141 ratings are not DA9091 ratings. No public DA9091 datasheet was found in the vendor search. A conventional but unconfirmed Renesas URL returned HTTP 404; that probe is recorded as a failed guess, not proof of nonexistence. [Attempted endpoint](https://www.renesas.com/en/document/dst/da9091-datasheet)

Web-index/rendered evidence available before those downloads is explicitly distinguished from locally hashed originals. Two paginated forum web requests also returned 403. No such official fault-survival statement was found in the inspected public sources.
