### Fault energy and surviving telemetry

**ASSUMED example, CALCULATED only:** a 24 V high-side buck short, initial rail 5.1 V, constant L=6.8 µH, 5 A initial current and zero initial excess capacitor current give `di/dt=(24−5.1)/L=2.779 A/µs`. Over 5.4 µs, current increases 15.009 A. Using the assumed 85.272 µF raw bank gives `ΔV≈(24−5.1)t²/(2LC)=0.475 V`. `½LI²` is 85 µJ initially and 1.361 mJ at the illustrative end current. [Inputs, formulas and results](protection_screening.json) distinguish this from a safe bound.

The true elapsed exposure includes travel to the latest threshold, propagation/deglitch, gate charge removal at minimum sink capability, Miller coupling and commutation. Saturation, source/eFuse response, capacitor bias collapse, ESR/ESL and cable inductance invalidate a simple universal bound. After opening, the raw capacitor bank stays on the buck side: it cannot be counted as Pi hold-up. Inductor current continues into raw capacitance/clamps and may raise disconnect VDS; **not all inductor energy necessarily reaches the load**. Evaluate raw-side overshoot, off-switch VDS/VGS and hot pulse SOA/avalanche separately from load `V(t)`, `I(t)`, charge and energy. Determine a clamp/absorption requirement from that result, not by placing an unselected TVS.

**KNOWN / INFERRED power-state consequences:** TPS709 IN is `VOUT_PROTECTED`; its operating input limit is 30 V. INA228 permits high analog bus voltage even without VS, but its low-voltage supply and the TCA9517 are not high-voltage rails. An upstream disconnect cannot remove an overvoltage source attached directly to `LOAD_5V1`. Define that external source's allowed voltage and local demand. With external LOAD live, local telemetry is supplied backwards through the shunt, and its demand is reported as negative branch current. With no external source after disconnection, local telemetry will eventually turn off; survival means no damage, not continuous reporting or retained accumulators. A detector supplied only from `3V3_LOCAL` could lose fault authority or restart cyclically. Its own supply/startup/fail-off design is an R2 blocker. Do not move INA228's supply or join host/local rails to solve this.

**Later validation, not demands for pre-PCB measurements:** actual board efficiency, heat/ripple/EMI, transient waveforms, fault let-through and harness temperatures belong to R5. Pre-layout needs accepted test limits and defensible design evidence; it cannot require measurements of a nonexistent board. Final fabrication exports and release are R4 tasks after completed R3 checks/review. Firmware calibration and continuous energy logging are later work; absence of a finished application is not independently a reason to delay PCB layout once its electrical interface is qualified.

## Proposed R2 closure plan — STOP before implementation

1. **Freeze the missing acceptance envelope.** Record load normal min/max, OV voltage-versus-time and energy/charge criteria; peak/slew/repetition; load C/ESR; input tolerance/surge/reverse/short-current/foldback; external LOAD range/leakage/handover; ambient/airflow/board/assembly constraints. Keep unknowns OPEN. Define whether telemetry must merely survive or remain powered after a trip. Propose output-OV latch with deliberate reset for review; do not silently adopt it.
2. **Approve a protection direction using that envelope.** Retain the reviewed chain. Evaluate the simplest controller/FET circuit that satisfies threshold, maximum delay and hot conduction/SOA. If a precision detector is required, return with exact supply/detector/driver/FET/divider/latch choices, power-state matrix and fault calculations before capture. Document why alternatives fail. No part is approved by this comparison.
3. **Close physical component/interface decisions.** Qualify U201/Q101/R401/L201/TDK footprints from authoritative drawings; choose F101 and J501/J502/J503, exact mates/wires and controlled passive ratings; resolve I²C low-level margin and thermal constraints. Package knowledge alone is not a footprint release.
4. **After explicit design approval, implement R2 only.** Capture the approved independent protection without bypassing it; update BOM, calculations, schematic review and source provenance. Preserve this restore point and all prior exports. Update `check_r2_schematic.py` and its tests to the new approved state: retain Kelvin/power-domain invariants, replace the historical open-protection assertion, and make the no-PCB assertion gate-specific when R3 is actually authorized. Neither assertion is a permanent architecture rule.
5. **Review complete R2 evidence, then authorize R3 separately.** Fresh ERC with explained outcomes, independent pin-to-net/footprint check, visual schematic review, corner calculations and a fault/state test plan must support the manager gate decision. Rule-check PASS alone never authorizes fabrication. No routing or Gerbers in this task.
