## OWNER EXPLANATION — one-page text handout

**We cannot approve either requested fault exposure for the captured board.** The eFuse chip can disconnect a 33.6 V source, but another part remains connected to that source and can overheat. A one-second 67.2 V exposure also exceeds published limits if it reaches the chip without a qualified clamp.

“The module trips first” sets the module's protection below V1A's earliest overvoltage trip. That keeps V1A out of its own input-fault state, provided the module's complete fault peak is bounded. “V1A survives and trips itself” allows the module's protection to act later. That can be acceptable: V1A disconnects its buck while its incoming components safely tolerate the source. It needs proof of both incoming-component survival and the voltage/energy that passes downstream before disconnection. A trip threshold alone proves neither.

The Pi needs its **5 V rail to stay below 5.5 V**, including fault pulses, and controlled loss/recovery of power. It does not require a particular order between two upstream trip detectors. Surviving a fault can mean safely turning the Pi off; continued operation needs a separate hold-up requirement. V1A's own buck-failure protection still has unresolved engineering gates, so a safe auxiliary converter would not by itself qualify the Pi interface.

Here the TVS is a shunt connected before the eFuse. Opening the eFuse removes the buck load but leaves the TVS energized. Its published standoff is **26 V**; at the specified room-temperature test its breakdown is **28.9–31.9 V**. A maintained **33.6 V** drives it into avalanche. Heating depends on available current and cooling, both **OPEN**. Its **600 W** headline concerns a brief pulse, not continuous power. The unspecified fuse cannot be assumed to clear before the TVS overheats.

TI specifies **60 V** as the eFuse's maximum operating input, **67 V** as its ordinary absolute maximum, and **75 V for only 10 ms** under stated conditions. A **67.2 V, 1 s** pulse receives no exemption because the switch is off. Auxiliary supplies connected before the eFuse are also exposed. A TVS may reduce their voltage, but that needs a current, duration and energy proof.

Isolation still matters. Separate transformer windings provide no direct DC path from a shorted primary switch to the secondary. Feedback failure and coupled pulses can still produce secondary overvoltage; insulation damage needs separate fault analysis. Isolation and output containment address different failure paths.

For the next enquiry, use one proposed trim setting: **23.0 V (ASSUMED)**, with a supplier-proved complete error/ripple/startup budget below the normal 24 V ceiling. Retire the earlier 23.5 V proposal for this screen. Keep all four modules unapproved; the **TEP 200-7215UIR** remains the best engineering enquiry candidate if independent protection ahead of V1A and its mounting/cooling burden are accepted.

### D101 continuous heating and the 1 s transient

E1's current published revision specifies **5.0 W on an infinite heat sink with lead temperature held at 50°C**, **20°C/W typical junction-to-lead**, **100°C/W typical junction-to-ambient**, and **150°C maximum operating junction temperature**. These conditions matter: the **5 W** value is not an unconditional PCB continuous dissipation rating. **600 W at 10/1000 µs** uses a stated pad arrangement and initial-temperature derating; **800 W** is for marked stacked-die members, not SMBJ26CA. [Littelfuse SMBJ, pp.1–4](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/tvs-diodes-smbj-series-datasheet?assetguid=ba555e99-a12d-4f72-a0b6-86b06c67171e).

**CALCULATED:** at the specified **25°C** electrical test, **33.6 V exceeds maximum breakdown by 1.7 V**. The datasheet does not tabulate a guaranteed DC current at 33.6 V. Do not interpolate a guaranteed current from the pulse clamp point. Temperature changes breakdown; the table gives a maximum temperature coefficient **0.093%/°C** for this row, while the introductory formula uses a typical coefficient. Thus the room-temperature avalanche conclusion is not a guaranteed all-temperature I–V curve, and thermal feedback cannot be credited as protection.

For a maintained **ASSUMED 33.6 V at the TVS**, power is **CALCULATED `P = 33.6 × I_TVS`**. At **ASSUMED 0.10 A**, that is **3.36 W**; at **ASSUMED 1 A**, **33.6 W**. These are illustrative operating points, not predicted currents. Even an ideal **5 W** heat-sink allowance corresponds to only **0.149 A CALCULATED** at this voltage. A subampere source can overheat the TVS without clearing the unspecified nominal **5 A** fuse.

Using **ASSUMED** typical **100°C/W** as a screening thermal path, not a guarantee, gives **CALCULATED** maximum dissipation to reach the **150°C** junction ceiling of **1.25 W at 25°C ambient** or **0.90 W at 60°C ambient**; corresponding DC current screens at 33.6 V are **37.2 mA** and **26.8 mA**. Actual allowable current is **OPEN**, including thermal margin, copper, enclosure and hot initial state. Therefore the exact outcome is **conducts at the stated room-temperature condition; overheating is plausible and unbounded, not proved inevitable at every possible source impedance**. It cannot be accepted as continuous fault containment.

For the **ASSUMED 67.2 V / 1 s source test**, use `E_TVS = integral(V_TVS × I_TVS dt)` and the actual waveform. **CALCULATED:** **42.1 V × 14.3 A ≈602 W** is the pulse point, not a **602 J / 1 s** allowance. The source may be held near 67.2 V while the TVS node is lower; current is then set by source/cable/fuse impedance. A qualified clamp could prevent the IC seeing 67.2 V, but its **1 s hot energy/repetition rating and fuse clearing** are **OPEN**. One must show TVS survival until a rated upstream disconnect clears, including inductive overshoot; neither U101 OVP nor U101 current limiting removes this shunt current.

### Divider arithmetic and reproducibility

**ASSUMED** independent initial resistor tolerance **±0.1%** and **DATASHEET E2 ±150 nA** pin leakage, positive into the comparator. For each top/bottom pair:

```text
Vpin = (Vin/Rtop - Ileak) / (1/Rtop + 1/Rbottom)
Ptop = (Vin - Vpin)^2 / Rtop; Pbottom = Vpin^2 / Rbottom
Vin_trip = Vthreshold × (1 + Rtop/Rbottom) + Ileak × Rtop
```

## Revised buyer requirements and trim conflict resolution

### S4 — replacement requirement

**REQUIREMENT (this study): the module's guaranteed maximum OVP threshold plus its complete positive overshoot must be below V1A's proved withstand, with margin.** The module need not trip before V1A's eFuse. Proving S4 requires the complete source-side assembly and the transition at the protected output, not just the chip's nominal voltage rating.

**ASSUMED procurement margin:** **1.0 V** of additional voltage headroom after all guaranteed errors and overshoot. **OPEN:** owner acceptance and any greater temperature/aging/uncertainty margin needed. Require:

```text
OVP_module,max + ΔVdelay/store/commutation/harness,max + 1.0 V <= Wproven(qualified waveform)
Vheld,max + 1.0 V <= Wcontinuous,proven             if the faulty source can remain on
```

`Wproven` must include duration, temperature, current/energy, repetition and initially charged nodes. If protection permits only a bounded brief event, use a separately proved transient envelope; a brief pulse need not be compared with a continuous no-avalanche screen. If it can retry or remain energized, its repetition or continuous envelope must also be proved. **OPEN:** both envelopes for this board. **26 V DATASHEET standoff is only a room-temperature component screen; 28.9 V breakdown minimum, 42.1 V clamp, 60 V chip operating maximum and 67 V absolute maximum are not interchangeable board withstand numbers.**

Require independent containment that remains effective after the initiating module fault, including a failed switching/control element, with a reviewed latch/re-arm policy. Also bound `VIN_PROTECTED` and the Pi rail during OVP entry/recovery. The frozen rule that battery/regen voltage must never reach V1A remains; this change does not authorize **54.6 V** at V1A or transfer the module's primary surge rating to its secondary.

For a module **plus** an independent upstream secondary cutoff, record the module's internal OVP and the added cutoff's threshold/complete terminal peak separately. A protective accessory does not lower the module's internal OVP or make the standalone module pass S4. The protected assembly would need qualification against its actual V1A-terminal fault envelope and an explicitly documented buyer specification.
