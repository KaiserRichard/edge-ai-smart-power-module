## OWNER EXPLANATION — one-page handout

The robot battery reaches 54.6 V when full, and braking or plugging it in can drive the bus higher. V1A expects a much lower supply. Its input eFuse can disconnect an overvoltage, but it is not a device that holds the incoming connector at a safe voltage. We therefore need the purchased converter and its upstream/downstream protection to contain the battery fault before it reaches V1A.

An ordinary nonisolated buck has a switch connecting the battery to an inductor and then the output. If that switch welds short, the inductor eventually acts like a wire for DC. A “48 V to 24 V” label describes healthy operation; this fault can put the battery voltage on the supposedly 24 V wire. Software, a fuse sized for ordinary load current, and the failed switch's own controller cannot reliably stop it. A fuse can leave a dangerous voltage present when the fault current is too small to clear it.

An isolated converter transfers energy between separate transformer windings. **The transformer passes no sustained DC and provides no conductive connection between its windings.** With intact insulation, a shorted primary switch cannot create the buck's direct battery-positive-to-output path. A winding held at DC has a brief magnetic transient and then saturates; primary current can become destructive, so a suitable input fuse/protection is still essential. Isolation changes the failure path; it does not make every failure harmless. A feedback failure can keep transferring energy and raise the secondary voltage, a switching transient can couple through capacitance, and damaged insulation or incorrect wiring can bypass the separation. Supplier single-fault evidence and an independent output cutoff are needed. Bonding the two returns removes system galvanic isolation, although that bond alone does not create a positive-rail pass-through; all cables, shields, chassis and control ports must be reviewed.

V1A's input OVP begins tripping as low as 26.97 V in the frozen calculation. We want the upstream protection to act first, while leaving room for its own tolerance and the voltage gained during its response. The proposed upstream trip is **25.0 V ±2%**, so the latest allowed trip is **25.5 V**, with the complete fault peak limited to **26.0 V**. That leaves approximately **0.97 V** below V1A's earliest calculated trip. These are proposed allocations, not measured performance; full eFuse resistor drift still needs review. “OVP at 30 V” or an unspecified “OVP” feature does not meet this contract. An OVP threshold also does not prove that a delayed shutdown prevents overshoot. A converter whose OVP only turns off its failed power switch needs an independent disconnect or other qualified containment path.

A nominal 24 V output has another catch: positive tolerance, ripple and startup overshoot can exceed V1A's frozen 24 V **normal** limit even before any OVP trips. Buy a 24 V class unit with downward trim, provisionally set it to **23.5 V**, and verify the whole tolerance/transient budget. Other devices sharing the supply must accept that setting. Do not raise V1A's input specification simply because its eFuse trips later.

V1A needs approximately **28.33 W**, assuming 90% efficiency. Adding 50 W or 100 W gives **78.33 W** or **128.33 W** of continuous demand. With the proposed reserve, these need about **100 W usable** or **180–200 W class** capacity respectively, after trim and temperature derating. More power means larger size, cost, heat and possible fault current. Isolation also costs space and usually some efficiency, but removes a specific dangerous DC path. A wide input range costs more and gives useful bus-transient headroom; it does not replace a robot regeneration energy sink. The defensible purchase is a documented isolated unit plus proven output protection, with the unknown loads, temperature and bus peaks closed before approval.

## Cheap unbranded 48 V → 24 V buck modules

**DECISION: unacceptable for this robot-to-V1A interface under the frozen fault requirement.** A low price or ordinary bench regulation test supplies no evidence of transformer isolation, a guaranteed bus-transient rating, independent OVP threshold/tolerance/delay, hot-load derating, or switch-failure containment. In a common nonisolated buck, one shorted high-side switch creates precisely the forbidden DC pass-through path. A battery fuse can remain intact because overvoltage need not draw enough current to clear it. V1A's eFuse is not an upstream rail clamp and cannot be credited with surviving the unspecified primary fault.

This conclusion is about the missing architecture and evidence, not the marketplace name. An identified, traceable product with authoritative specifications and independently qualified protection could be re-evaluated. An unbranded unit could be used for an isolated, current-limited **dummy-load experiment** if separately authorized; passing that experiment would not qualify it for the robot or Pi. Shopee/AliExpress marketing text is not evidence of single-fault containment.
