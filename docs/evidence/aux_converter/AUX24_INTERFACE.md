## OWNER EXPLANATION — one-page handout

The robot battery reaches 54.6 V when full; braking or plugging it in can drive the bus higher. V1A expects a lower supply. Its eFuse can disconnect an overvoltage, but cannot hold the incoming connector at a safe voltage. The purchased converter and associated protection must contain the battery fault before it reaches V1A.

An ordinary nonisolated buck connects the battery through a switch and inductor to its output. If the switch welds short, the inductor eventually acts like a wire for DC. The battery voltage can then reach the supposedly 24 V wire. Software and the failed switch's controller cannot stop that conductive path. A fuse can leave the dangerous voltage present when current is too small to clear it.

An isolated converter transfers energy between separate transformer windings. **The transformer passes no sustained DC and has no conductive connection between its windings.** With intact insulation, a shorted primary switch cannot create a direct battery-positive-to-output path. A winding held at DC has a brief magnetic transient and then saturates; primary current can become destructive, so input protection is essential. A feedback failure can still raise the secondary voltage; switching transients can couple through capacitance, and damaged insulation or wiring can bypass separation. Supplier single-fault evidence and an independent output cutoff are needed. Bonding the returns removes system galvanic isolation, although that bond alone does not create positive-rail pass-through. Cables, shields, chassis and control ports need review.

V1A's OVP can trip as low as 26.97 V in the frozen calculation. Upstream protection should act first, allowing for tolerance and voltage gained during its response. The proposed trip is **25.0 V ±2%**: latest trip **25.5 V**, complete fault peak **≤26.0 V**, leaving **0.97 V** below V1A's earliest calculated trip. These are proposed limits, not measurements; full eFuse resistor drift remains open. “OVP at 30 V” does not meet the contract. A threshold alone does not bound delayed-shutdown overshoot. If protection only commands the failed switch off, an independent disconnect or other qualified containment path is needed.

A nominal 24 V output can exceed V1A's 24 V **normal** limit through tolerance, ripple and overshoot. Buy a 24 V class unit with downward trim; the proposed **23.5 V** setting needs a verified complete error budget. A larger error needs a lower setting. Shared loads must accept it. The later eFuse trip does not expand the normal-input requirement.

V1A needs **28.33 W**, assuming 90% efficiency. Adding 50 W or 100 W gives **78.33 W** or **128.33 W** demand. With reserve, allow about **100 W usable** or **180–200 W class**, after trim/thermal derating. More power increases size, cost, heat and possible fault current. Isolation costs space and usually efficiency. Wider input rating adds headroom but cannot replace a regeneration energy sink. Close loads, temperature, bus peaks and output protection before approving purchase.

## Cheap unbranded 48 V → 24 V buck modules

**DECISION: unacceptable for this robot-to-V1A interface under the frozen fault requirement.** A low price or ordinary bench regulation test supplies no evidence of transformer isolation, a guaranteed bus-transient rating, independent OVP threshold/tolerance/delay, hot-load derating, or switch-failure containment. In a common nonisolated buck, one shorted high-side switch creates precisely the forbidden DC pass-through path. A battery fuse can remain intact because overvoltage need not draw enough current to clear it. V1A's eFuse is not an upstream rail clamp and cannot be credited with surviving the unspecified primary fault.

This conclusion is about the missing architecture and evidence, not the marketplace name. An identified, traceable product with authoritative specifications and independently qualified protection could be re-evaluated. An unbranded unit could be used for an isolated, current-limited **dummy-load experiment** if separately authorized; passing that experiment would not qualify it for the robot or Pi. Shopee/AliExpress marketing text is not evidence of single-fault containment.
